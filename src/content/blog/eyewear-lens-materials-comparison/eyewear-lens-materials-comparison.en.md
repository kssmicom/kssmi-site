## The short answer

**A lens substrate name alone does not guarantee UV400, polarization, filter category, or impact rating — those depend on the finished lens stack, coatings, and sample testing. At Kssmi, choosing the right custom lens requires three decisions in order: match the substrate (TAC, CR-39, nylon, PC, or Trivex) to your frame construction and climate; separate optical functions from surface coatings; and align your color plan with per-color lens MOQs.**

For brand owners sourcing custom sunglasses or optical frames, lens errors rarely show up in a product photo — they show up when CR-39 cracks around rimless screws, TAC delaminates in hot climates, or a mirror-coated colorway hits a 300-pair color minimum on a 100-pair frame split. Kssmi supplies non-prescription sun lenses and optical demo or blue-light-filtering lenses; we do not manufacture prescription (Rx) lenses.

## Nylon (PA) lenses: high clarity for rimless and luxury builds

**Nylon (polyamide) combines ultra-light weight and high optical clarity (Abbe ~52) with high shatter resistance, making it Kssmi's primary recommendation for rimless drill-mount and exposed-edge half-frame designs.**

Because nylon resists stress cracking around drilled holes and tension-cord grooves, it keeps exposed lens edges clean and safe in wear where CR-39 or TAC would fail. It pairs naturally with lightweight [titanium sunglasses](/product/titanium-sunglasses/), [carbon-fiber sunglasses](/product/carbon-fiber-sunglasses/), and [rimless sunglasses](/product/rimless-sunglasses/).

Two practical rules apply to nylon programs:

- **Process control matters as much as the resin.** Nylon's final optical quality depends on grade, moisture conditioning, thermal history, forming stress, and coating adhesion — so optical performance is always verified on the finished physical sample rather than assumed from the resin name.
- **Professional lens replacement.** For rimless drilled-lens frames, lens swaps or color changes should always be handled by an optician or eyewear technician, not by end consumers.

## CR-39 lenses: cast-resin optical clarity for full-rim frames

**CR-39 is a thermoset cast ADC resin and the clarity benchmark (Abbe ~58) for full-rim fashion sunglasses, offering crisp color reproduction and smooth solid or gradient dyeing.**

Produced through resin filtration, degassing, mold casting, and controlled curing, CR-39 excels in boutique [acetate sunglasses](/product/acetate-sunglasses/) and [metal sunglasses](/product/metal-sunglasses/) where optical crispness and classic tinted aesthetics lead. However, because cast CR-39 is stiffer and more vulnerable to stress cracking around screw holes, it should not be specified for rimless drill mounts or nylon-cord half-rim frames.

## TAC polarized lenses: composite laminate, not a standalone polarizer

**TAC (triacetate cellulose) itself does not polarize light — a TAC polarized lens is a multi-layer laminate uniting outer TAC layers, an oriented polarizing film, protective support layers, and adhesives.**

At Kssmi, TAC polarized is the cost-effective entry route for full-rim polarized sunwear and daytime driving lines. Because polarization comes from the laminated film axis and adhesive integrity rather than a single solid resin, layer sequence, edge sealing, and controlled forming are critical to prevent bubbles, axis drift, or delamination.

Keep two strict boundaries in mind before specifying TAC:

- **Full-rim frames only.** Never use TAC laminates in rimless or open-edge constructions where exposed edges can delaminate.
- **Climate limits.** TAC polarized lenses are not recommended for very hot, humid destination climates where prolonged heat stresses the adhesive stack; choose nylon or polycarbonate polarized structures instead for demanding heat exposure.

## Polycarbonate (PC) and Trivex: impact resistance, high wrap and shields

**Polycarbonate (PC) is the workhorse substrate for thermoformed high-wrap sport shields and drop-ball impact resistance, while Trivex (refractive index 1.53) delivers urethane-based impact protection paired with sharper optics.**

PC injection and thermoforming support aggressive wraparound geometries that flat-cut blanks cannot reach. As a confirmed engineering benchmark at Kssmi, we thermoform one-piece PC shield lenses up to a **7-base curve with a 151° included wrap**. Because wrapped shields are toric (horizontal and vertical curve radii differ) and thermoforming locks the finished lens wall thickness permanently, frame grooves and rim walls must be engineered to match the formed lens thickness — the lens cannot be thinned afterward. Also note that when sharing curved-lens dimensions, the developed arc length and straight-line chord width are distinct numbers that must both match the drawing.

For U.S.-market sport and protective programs that specify **Trivex** (index 1.53), Kssmi sources the urethane-based material to your confirmed specification — including clear plano lenses with broadband anti-reflective coating — and never substitutes polycarbonate in its place. ## Optical frames: demo lenses and non-prescription blue-light filtering

**Every optical frame from Kssmi ships with clear plano demo lenses ready for local optician Rx glazing, or with non-prescription blue-light-filtering lenses — we do not manufacture prescription (Rx) lenses.**

When ordering non-Rx screen eyewear, keep two technical distinctions clear:

- **Spectral design over marketing labels.** Blue-light filtering works by selectively attenuating target wavelength bands through material absorption, tinting, or coating. Always confirm the target wavelength band, blocking ratio, and base-tint warmth (clear base vs. light or solid yellow base) rather than relying on a generic "blue-blocking" label. Selective filtering changes spectral balance; it does not increase optical resolution or make health claims.
- **Separate product standards.** Unglazed optical frames intended for prescription fitting fall under **ISO 12870**, whereas general-use plano sunglasses fall under **EN ISO 12312-1** or regional sunwear standards. Fitting a demo lens does not make an optical frame subject to sunglass standards.

## Functions vs. coatings: what buyers often confuse

**Optical functions change what light passes through the lens (brightness, UV, glare, or spectrum); surface coatings change how the lens surface resists scratches, reflections, water, or fog — and Hard Coating (HC) and Anti-Reflective (AR) coating are not automatic defaults.**

### Optical functions (confirmed on the finished lens)

- **Solar filtering & VLT / CAT 0–4:** Visible light transmission (VLT) measures darkness instrumentally — darkness cannot be judged by eye. **CAT.3 (8–18% VLT)** is the global standard for general-purpose sunwear and daytime driving; **CAT.1 (43–80% VLT)** and **CAT.2 (18–43% VLT)** suit overcast wear, fashion tints, and gradients. Confirm destination driving rules before locking VLT.
- **UV400 protection:** UV filtration blocks UVA/UVB through substrate, additives, tint, or coating. A clear or light fashion lens can still achieve UV400, while a dark lens without UV absorbers does not — UV400 claims always require finished-lens test verification.
- **Polarization:** Attenuates horizontally reflected glare from water, roads, and snow. Polarization is independent of darkness, UV400, or mirror appearance; most [gray](/customization/lenses/grey/), [brown](/customization/lenses/brown/), and [green](/customization/lenses/green/) CAT.3 swatches support polarized stacks.
- **Photochromic & High Contrast (Hi-Con):** Photochromic systems darken under UV/spectral exposure, with transition speed and depth strongly affected by ambient temperature and vehicle windshields (which block UV). High-contrast tints shift spectral contrast under specific backgrounds without changing Abbe value or geometric resolution.

### Surface treatments & edge finishes (specify explicitly in your RFQ)

- **Hard Coating (HC) & Anti-Reflective (AR):** State HC and AR explicitly when requesting a quote; for balanced premium sunwear, **UV400 + HC** is a standard pairing. On flat 2-base fashion lenses or dark mirror lenses, add an **inner backside AR coating** to stop bounce-back eye-side glare.
- **Mirror / Flash Coating:** Outer metallic or oxide layers create a reflective look, but **"heavier mirror" does not automatically mean darker VLT or CAT.3–4**. At Kssmi, mirror coatings run on a **full-surface coater** that coats front and back simultaneously without directional sweep marks, keeping mirror color and thickness uniform from center to edge even on wide wraparound shields. See [mirror-coated lens options](/customization/lenses/mirror-coated/).
- **Hydrophobic/Oleophobic (H/O) & Anti-Fog (AF):** H/O repels water and fingerprints as an outermost top coat; AF makes condensation form a clear film rather than droplets. Because hydrophobic and hydrophilic (anti-fog) mechanisms oppose each other, specify only the surface priority your use case requires.
- **Edge treatments & stress checks:** Standard rimless lenses use a **standard polished bevel edge** at standard pricing with no setup fee. A **faceted (diamond-cut) edge** — step-cut pass by pass on the edging machine — carries a one-time **USD 350** machine setup fee plus higher per-pair edging cost. Because thermal bending, edging, and tight frame mounting can introduce **stress birefringence**, Kssmi checks optical wavefront and stress condition after assembly on approved samples.

## How to choose the right lens for your collection

Match the lens substrate and finish to your frame construction, target climate, and launch volume:

- **Rimless or exposed-edge frames:** Specify **nylon (PA)** or **polycarbonate (PC)**. Do not use **CR-39** or **TAC**, because drilling or exposed edges crack CR-39 and invite moisture delamination into TAC layers.
- **Hot or humid destination climates:** Avoid **TAC** polarized laminates for long-term exposure; choose **nylon polarized** or **PC polarized** instead.
- **Full-rim fashion or vintage acetate sunglasses:** Choose **CR-39** for optical clarity (Abbe ~58) or **nylon** when you want lighter weight and higher impact safety.
- **One-piece sport shields or high-wrap designs:** Choose **thermoformed PC** (supporting 7-base / 151° shield curvature) with **full-surface vacuum coater mirror plating**, and lock both **curved arc length** and **straight chord width** on the drawing.
- **Lean 300-pair / 3-colorway launches:** Use **solid standard catalog colors** (100 pairs per color) rather than mirror coatings (300 pairs per color) or custom tints (300 pairs per color), and specify **HC** and **backside AR** explicitly in your RFQ because neither is added by default.

## FAQ

### 1. Which lens material works best for rimless sunglasses?

Choose nylon (PA) or polycarbonate (PC) for rimless drill-mount and half-rim tension-cord designs. Standard CR-39 and TAC films are vulnerable to stress cracking or edge delamination around drill holes, so they are not specified for rimless constructions.

### 2. What is the difference between TAC polarized, PC, and nylon lenses?

TAC polarized is a multi-layer laminated film around an oriented polarizing layer, restricted to full-rim frames and not preferred for very hot climates. PC is injection-molded or thermoformed for high impact resistance and deep sport wraps. Nylon delivers ultra-light weight, high optical clarity, and drill-mount strength across luxury and rimless frames.

### 3. Which lens material offers the clearest optics?

CR-39 is the clarity benchmark with an Abbe value around 58, while nylon (PA) also offers high clarity (Abbe ~52) at lower weight. However, finished visual clarity also depends on base curve, coating stack, and assembly stress, so final optical quality is always verified on the approved sample.

### 4. What MOQs apply to lens colors, and do they add to frame minimums?

Yes. Lens color minimums sit on top of frame MOQs and are counted per color: solid standard catalog colors start at 100 pairs, gradient tints at 150 pairs (around USD 0.60 more per pair than solid), and mirror-coated or custom non-catalog colors at 300 pairs per color. A mirror-coated colorway therefore starts at 300 pairs even on a 300-pair / 3-color frame order.

### 5. Do dark tints or heavy mirror coatings automatically mean UV400 or CAT.3?

No. Visible darkness (VLT / CAT), mirror reflectivity, polarization, and UV400 filtration are separate optical properties. A light lens can block UV400 if formulated for it, while a heavy mirror coating does not guarantee CAT.3 darkness or UV protection; every metric must be verified on the finished lens.

### 6. Are Hard Coating (HC) and inner Anti-Reflective (AR) coating included by default?

No. Hard coating and anti-reflective coating are not automatic defaults and must be stated in your quotation request. For premium sunwear, UV400 with HC is a common baseline, and flat 2-base or dark mirror lenses benefit from an inner backside AR coating to eliminate bounce-back glare.

### 7. How should lenses be specified for one-piece wraparound sport shields?

Specify thermoformed polycarbonate (PC) or suitable nylon grades, and confirm both the developed arc length and straight-line chord width on the drawing. Kssmi thermoforms shields up to a 7-base curve with a 151° wrap and uses a full-surface vacuum coater so mirror finishes remain uniform from center to edge.

### 8. What is the difference between a standard bevel edge and a faceted diamond-cut edge?

A standard polished bevel edge has a clean single bevel at standard lens pricing with no setup charge. A faceted (diamond-cut) edge cuts multi-facet jewelry-style steps pass by pass, adding a one-time USD 350 machine setup charge plus a higher per-pair edging cost.

### 9. Can I order spare replacement lenses with my eyewear production?

Yes. Replacement lenses can be added as a separate line item alongside a confirmed eyewear production order, starting at a 100-piece minimum and billed separately. For rimless frames, lens replacement must be performed by a professional optician rather than end wearers.

### 10. Do you supply prescription lenses, and do optical frames use the same standard as sunglasses?

No, Kssmi does not produce prescription (Rx) lenses; optical frames ship with clear demo lenses or non-Rx blue-light-filtering lenses. Unglazed optical frames for Rx fitting fall under ISO 12870, which is separate from finished plano sunglass standards such as EN ISO 12312-1.

### 11. How are spectral curves and third-party compliance tests handled?

Kssmi provides factory test references free of charge and can supply 280–780 nm spectral transmission data (5 nm steps) and raw CSV files for tested formulations. Independent third-party testing under EN ISO 12312-1, ANSI Z80.3, AS/NZS 1067.1, or FDA 21 CFR 801.410 is coordinated through labs like SGS, Intertek, or TÜV at actual lab cost and applies to the tested finished SKU.
