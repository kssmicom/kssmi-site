## The short answer

**Frame material is not just an aesthetic choice — it sets your construction route, minimum order quantity (200, 300, or 1,200 pairs per model), tooling commitment, and which shapes or hardware details are physically possible.**

At Kssmi, six frame material routes cover nearly all custom eyewear programs: cellulose acetate, 316 stainless steel, nickel silver (copper–nickel–zinc alloy), titanium (pure and beta), carbon fiber, and TR-90 injection. Choosing the wrong material for a reference shape — such as asking for a sculpted, wraparound bubble silhouette in acetate sheet — delays quoting and sampling before production even starts.

## Acetate frames: color depth, finishing, and shape limits

**Cellulose acetate is a plant-based thermoplastic cut from layered sheets, ideal for rich color depth and hand-polished edges.**

Our acetate frames go through a five-stage finishing sequence: sheet sanding, barrel/tumble rough polishing, high-gloss fine polishing, hand finishing and edge refinement, and a pre-assembly quality check. Acetate temples normally require an internal metal wire core for structural strength and fitting adjustment. Half-frame designs with an exposed lower lens edge can also be built in acetate by securing the lens with a fine transparent cord seated into a precision-carved groove.

Three engineering and sourcing boundaries matter before you lock an acetate design:

1. **Shape and base-curve limits.** For a TR-90-style wraparound shield silhouette, an acetate version requires an additional vertical support at the bridge. It cannot copy the same uninterrupted shield construction; confirm the adapted bridge structure on the drawing before sampling.
2. **Built-in spring hinges.** When buyers ask for a "hidden hinge" in acetate, the workable construction is a **built-in spring hinge housed inside the acetate temple** with the small mechanical clearance required for the arm to swing. Zero-clearance total concealment is physically impossible, and on translucent acetate the internal mechanism remains partly visible.
3. **Finished-frame MOQ vs. raw-material MOQ.** Finished acetate orders start at 300 pairs per model (up to 3 colors × 100 pairs). With top-tier Chinese mills (JIMEI / JINYU), each color starts from 100 pairs. Italian Mazzucchelli sheet carries a raw-material purchasing threshold equivalent to roughly 1,000 pairs of one style in one color, so a 300-pair first batch in Italian acetate effectively runs one color per style. As a pricing guide, Mazzucchelli typically runs roughly **USD 3–5 more per pair** in standard colors (and **USD 5–10 more per pair** in tortoiseshell patterns) than standard acetate, confirmed in the quotation. In addition, acetate cannot be stored indefinitely without risking color shift or brittleness, so any reserved sheet should be scheduled into production within a reasonable window.

## Metal frames: stainless steel vs. nickel silver

**316 stainless steel suits clean, slim everyday profiles, while nickel silver (copper–nickel–zinc alloy) is the workable cost-down metal route when your design relies on cast decorative ornaments.**

Both metals follow the standard 300-pair-per-model minimum (up to 3 colors × 100 pairs) and support 18 PVD plated color options, including gold, rose gold, silver/chrome and gunmetal. However, they behave differently in tooling and detail:

- **316 Stainless steel** delivers slim, structured fronts and adjustable temples at a practical cost tier, and pairs well with acetate or TR-90 temples in mixed-material builds. It does not reproduce fine cast ornaments cleanly.
- **Nickel silver (copper–nickel–zinc alloy)** machines well, takes gold plating cleanly and holds crisp cast ornament details (such as geometric brow or temple emblems). Its metal components are roughly 1.9× denser than titanium (≈8.7 vs. ≈4.5 g/cm³), so the frame feels noticeably heavier than titanium while avoiding stainless steel's casting limits.

Finish choice also drives cost and lead time. Standard PVD colors run on normal schedules, whereas large mirror-gloss plated surfaces require a multi-pass plating cycle (normally 2–3 passes, occasionally more, at roughly **USD 0.60 per pair per pass** as a pricing guide, with the final pass count confirmed in the quotation) to stay uniform, raising unit cost, MOQ and production time.

## Titanium frames: pure titanium vs. beta titanium

**Pure titanium and beta titanium deliver featherweight comfort and corrosion resistance, with beta titanium offering higher elasticity and spring-back for thinner, high-demand profiles.**

While pure titanium is valued for low weight and corrosion resistance, **beta titanium** is an alloy engineered for greater tensile strength and elastic recovery, making it the more common choice for modern premium frames with slim temples or flexible bridges. As a pricing guide, titanium runs approximately **USD 3.70 more per pair** than stainless steel, with the exact difference confirmed in the quotation according to the design, grade and component set.

Two engineering notes apply to titanium projects:

- **Screwless, solderless construction.** Titanium frames can be engineered with mechanical interlocking joinery — no screws and no welding. Because each interlocking mechanism depends on rim thickness, hinge geometry and lens retention, exact feasibility is confirmed per model during engineering review and 2D drawing approval.
- **Metal-contact specifications.** Titanium is widely chosen for comfort-focused lines. From a procurement standpoint, confirm plating, solder alloys and any nickel content against the approved sample and your destination market's regulations. This guide makes no medical or skin-contact claims for end wearers.

## Carbon fiber frames

**Carbon fiber offers the lowest per-model minimum in our lineup (200 pairs per model) alongside ultra-light stiffness and a woven technical look.**

Built from layered carbon fiber weave, carbon frames hold rigid geometry at minimal weight and pair cleanly with PVD-plated metal hardware or structured fronts. Because the weave pattern is part of the structural surface, color variation typically comes from finish tone, temple accents and lens tints rather than opaque sheet colors.

## TR-90 and injection-molded frames

**TR-90 is a flexible thermoplastic nylon built in dedicated injection molds — essential for wraparound sport shields, sculpted 3D curves and impact-resistant volume lines, with a 1,200-pair standard MOQ.**

Unlike CNC-cut acetate or formed metal, TR-90 shapes are injected directly into steel production molds. That unlocks aggressive wrap geometry, 3D contoured temples and lightweight impact resistance for sport-focused designs. Sport TR-90 projects can also start from our dedicated sport design templates rather than from zero.

Two commercial rules govern TR-90:

1. **Dedicated tooling and higher MOQ.** Every TR-90 model, including custom sport models, requires at least 1,200 pairs per model and at least 600 pairs per color. Reusing an existing shape saves new tooling, not either minimum.
2. **Pre-tooling verification and fee credits.** For custom injection shapes, fit and ergonomics are normally verified before cutting production steel molds, and TR-90 injection tooling and sample fees are credited once the same model reaches a cumulative **30,000 pairs within 24 months** (excluding freight and taxes), as stated in the quotation.

## Hybrid frames, rimless builds, hinges and lenses

**Components do not sit in isolation: a metal front with acetate temples, a drilled rimless build, hinge barrel count and lens color minimums all reshape your final MOQ and tooling plan.**

- **Hybrid constructions.** Combining a metal front with acetate or TR-90 temples balances slim front styling with warmer temple grip. Any change in material or construction creates a distinct model with its own MOQ.
- **Rimless (drilled-lens) feasibility.** Rimless builds are standard in metal and titanium, and are also technically feasible in acetate and TR-90. However, acetate and TR-90 drilled builds do not match the look of a gold-plated metal frame with cast ornaments, so we confirm aesthetic fit honestly before sampling. For rimless or exposed-edge half-frame designs, **nylon lenses** are recommended for edge durability and shatter resistance, and lens swaps should be handled by an optician.
- **Hinges and hardware.** We offer 3-barrel, 5-barrel and 7-barrel hinges as a matched system with your frame specification. At standard 300-pair quantities, we use precision Shenzhen hinges with equivalent performance to international brands; international-brand hinges (when specified) carry their own supply minimum of roughly 3,000 pairs per model.
- **Lens minimums sit on top of frame minimums.** Sunglasses use UV400 lenses in TAC, CR-39, PC or nylon (with Trivex available on specification). Solid standard lens colors start at 100 pairs per color, gradient tints at 150 pairs per color, and mirror-coated or custom non-catalog colors at 300 pairs per color. A mirror-coated colorway therefore requires 300 pairs even when the frame route allows 100 pairs per color.

## How to choose the right material for your collection

1. **Check physical shape feasibility first.** For a TR-90-style wraparound shield silhouette, an acetate version requires an additional vertical support at the bridge.
2. **Match weight to wearing duration.** All-day optical wearers and active sport buyers feel every gram — favor beta titanium, pure titanium, carbon fiber or TR-90. Statement fashion sunglasses carry solid acetate well.
3. **Align color splits with raw-material rules.** If you want three colorways of 100 pairs on a 300-pair launch, choose metal, titanium, or JIMEI/JINYU acetate — not Italian Mazzucchelli sheet or TR-90.
4. **Match hardware and plating to your price tier.** Choose nickel silver over stainless steel if your design includes fine cast ornaments; budget extra cost and lead time for multi-pass mirror plating.
5. **Confirm tooling recovery before locking volume.** Only commit to custom molds when your 12-month forecast supports repeating a non-TR-90 style (or your 24-month forecast supports a TR-90 style).

## FAQ

### 1. Which eyewear frame material is the lightest?

Carbon fiber and titanium (pure or beta titanium) are the lightest frame options in our range, followed closely by TR-90. Exact frame weight depends on front thickness, rim profile, hardware and lens size, and is verified against the approved physical sample.

### 2. Can sculpted or bubble-shaped frames be made in cellulose acetate?

For a TR-90-style wraparound shield silhouette, an acetate version requires an additional vertical support at the bridge. It cannot copy the same uninterrupted shield construction; confirm the adapted bridge structure on the drawing before sampling.

### 3. What is the difference between pure titanium and beta titanium?

Pure titanium is lightweight and corrosion-resistant. Beta titanium is a titanium alloy with higher strength and elasticity, giving better spring-back for thin temples and demanding profiles, which makes it the more common choice for modern premium frames.

### 4. When should a brand choose nickel silver instead of stainless steel?

Choose nickel silver (copper–nickel–zinc alloy) when your design requires fine cast decorative ornaments and gold plating at a lower cost than titanium. Stainless steel suits clean, slim profiles but cannot reproduce sharp cast ornament details; note that nickel silver metal parts are roughly 1.9 times denser than titanium.

### 5. How do frame material and finish choices affect unit cost?

At Kssmi, pricing guides follow material and finishing complexity: titanium runs roughly USD 3.70 more per pair than stainless steel; Italian Mazzucchelli acetate runs roughly USD 3–5 more per pair in standard colors (and USD 5–10 more in tortoiseshell patterns) than standard acetate; and large multi-pass mirror plating runs roughly USD 0.60 per pair per pass across 2–3 or more passes. For metal builds with cast ornaments, nickel silver lowers cost compared with titanium, while exact unit pricing is confirmed in the project quotation after engineering review.

### 6. Can you build completely concealed hinges inside acetate frames?

Zero-clearance, completely concealed hinges are physically impossible because a hinge needs mechanical clearance to swing. The workable premium solution is a built-in spring hinge housed inside the acetate temple, with the internal seating shown on the 2D drawing before tooling; in translucent acetate, the hinge remains partly visible.

### 7. Why does Italian acetate affect my first-order color split?

Finished acetate frames start at 300 pairs per model (up to 3 colors at 100 pairs each). However, Italian Mazzucchelli sheet carries a raw-material minimum equal to roughly 1,000 pairs of one style in one color, making a 300-pair first batch effectively one color per style. Top-tier Chinese acetate mills (JIMEI / JINYU) allow 100 pairs per color.

### 8. What MOQ applies to each frame material, and do lens colors add extra minimums?

Acetate, metal (stainless steel and nickel silver) and titanium start at 300 pairs per model; carbon fiber starts at 200 pairs per model; TR-90 requires at least 1,200 pairs per model and at least 600 pairs per color. Lens color minimums sit on top: solid standard tints start at 100 pairs, gradient at 150, and mirror-coated or custom tints at 300 pairs per color.

### 9. How long do custom sampling and bulk production take?

Technical drawings take 3–10 working days. A fully custom sample, including tooling where required, takes 60 working days after drawing approval and full fee payment. Bulk production takes 60 working days after sample approval and receipt of the 50% deposit, across all materials including TR-90.

### 10. Can one frame combine different materials or use rimless construction?

Yes. Hybrid builds — such as a metal front with acetate or TR-90 temples — are standard, and rimless drilled-lens builds work in metal, titanium, acetate and TR-90 (with nylon lenses recommended for exposed edges). Each material or construction change creates a separate model with its own MOQ and tooling review.

### 11. Do you supply retail packaging or prescription lenses?

No. We do not manufacture or supply retail packaging, though we pack finished frames into customer-supplied packaging at no extra charge. We do not produce prescription lenses; optical frames ship with demo lenses.
