## The short answer

**OEM means we manufacture to your confirmed design. ODM means we develop the design from your direction. Private label is not a third design route: it describes whose brand goes on the finished frame, and it can sit on a catalogue-based model or a fully custom one.**

Two things follow from that. The OEM or ODM label alone does not set your minimum order, tooling cost or lead time — the construction route does. And buyers use these words loosely, so the route is confirmed in writing before anything is quoted.

## Who designs, and whose brand goes on it

OEM and ODM answer one question: who originates the design. Private label answers a different question: whose brand is on the product. Mixing the three into a single list is where most confusion starts.

| | OEM | ODM |
|---|---|---|
| Design origin | You | Kssmi's design and engineering team, working from your direction |
| What you send | A confirmed specification, technical drawing, or a sample you own | A sketch, photo, reference, mood board or AI-generated concept |
| What we do | Engineering review, tooling where required, sampling, production, quality control | Design development, 2D drawings, tooling where required, sampling, production |
| What you approve | Drawings, then the physical sample | Drawings, then the physical sample |
| Branding | Your brand | Your brand |

Private label adds the branding layer rather than replacing the design route. A catalogue-based private-label project starts from an existing construction, which usually means no new tooling; a custom private-label project is OEM or ODM work carrying your brand. In both cases every production pair is made for your order. We keep samples for evaluation, but we do not sell stock eyewear.

## What actually drives cost and lead time

Four variables decide the quotation, and none of them is the label itself:

1. **Whether the construction already exists.** A catalogue-based model reuses existing tooling. A new shape needs a new mold, which is the largest one-time cost in most projects.
2. **Who produces the design.** OEM with a finished specification skips design development. ODM includes it, which is why the drawing stage appears in the schedule.
3. **How many models and colours.** The minimum is per model, not per order. Five models mean five minimums.
4. **Branding, components and lenses.** Logo method, custom hardware, lens type and lens colour each carry their own thresholds and costs.

This is why two projects both described as "OEM" can quote very differently, and why the first useful question is not "OEM or ODM?" but "what do you already have?"

## Route by what you already have

- **A technical drawing or complete specification** → OEM. Engineering review and quotation come first, then 2D confirmation, tooling where required, and a physical sample.
- **An existing model you want to adapt** → Development from a known construction. Faster than starting from zero, but changing the geometry, material or construction can make it a new model with its own tooling and minimum.
- **A sketch, photo, mood board or AI-generated concept** → ODM. We turn the direction into a 2D drawing, then a physical sample, then production. You do not need CAD.
- **A physical sample or a frame you own** → Custom development. We review the construction, materials and what can be reproduced lawfully. If the reference construction cannot be made in the material you want, we quote the workable material route rather than an impossible one. Send only designs you are entitled to use.
- **Nothing yet beyond a market and a price point** → Start from catalogue references. Choose shapes to evaluate, then confirm whether the project stays catalogue-based or becomes a new custom model.

## OEM in practice

**In OEM, the factory's job is production, not design.** You bring the approved design; we handle materials, tooling, assembly, quality control and packing.

A workable OEM specification covers frame dimensions and fit, material and colour, lens material and function, logo artwork and placement, hinges and hardware, and quantity per model and colour. If the specification is incomplete, the gaps are resolved through sample rounds, which costs more calendar time than resolving them on paper.

The sequence is: engineering review and quotation, then 2D confirmation with up to three revision rounds, then tooling where required, then a physical sample, then bulk production after sample approval.

For a fully custom project based on your design, the 2D drawings, renderings and production drawings belong to you once the related development fees are paid. They are not reused for another customer. Fully paid tooling is also yours, and is used only for your confirmed project.

## ODM in practice

**In ODM, you brief, we design, you approve — and you do not need a designer or CAD file to start.** A sketch, a product photo, a mood board or an AI-generated concept is enough for engineering review.

A brief that produces a usable first drawing covers six things:

1. Frame type — sunglasses, optical, rimless, sport or hybrid.
2. Material direction — acetate, titanium, metal, carbon fibre or TR-90.
3. Lens requirements — category, tint, polarization, photochromic or blue-light filtering.
4. Target market and retail price — these drive material and hardware choices more than anything else.
5. Branding — logo artwork, method and placement.
6. Quantity per model and target launch date.

ODM runs the same gates as OEM: a 2D technical drawing in 3–10 working days, up to three revision rounds, your written approval, then tooling and a physical sample, then bulk production.

Two boundaries matter. Reference images guide an original design; they are not a licence to copy another brand's frame, and we ask you to supply only designs and logos you are entitled to use. And design origin does not decide ownership: for custom work developed from your brief, the drawings belong to you once the development fees are paid.

## Catalogue-based private label

**Private label means your brand on a confirmed specification. When the specification is based on an existing catalogue construction, no new tooling is needed — but it is still production made for your order, not a stock sale.**

This is the shortest development path. A stock evaluation sample takes 2–5 working days; with a standard logo added, about 10 working days, at US$15 per model. Nothing else on that sample can be changed, and availability is confirmed per model. Once you approve the direction, bulk production follows the standard per-model minimum and the normal production schedule.

What this route removes is design development and new tooling. It does not remove the per-model minimum, and it does not create exclusivity: the same catalogue construction remains available to other buyers. That is the trade — speed and lower setup cost in exchange for a shape you do not own.

## MOQ: the question behind most first orders

**The minimum is per model, not per order.** A 300-pair model can be split into three colourways of 100 pairs; three colours is an option, not a requirement. Five models at 300 pairs each is 1,500 pairs, because each model carries its own development and production setup.

| Route | Minimum | Colour split |
|---|---|---|
| Acetate, metal and titanium, standard construction | 300 pairs per model | Up to 3 colours × 100 pairs |
| Carbon fibre | 200 pairs per model | Confirmed with the project |
| TR-90 injection | 1,200 pairs per style | At least 600 pairs per colour |

Rules that are easier to know now than later:

- Quantities are **not pooled across models**, and a first partial batch is not produced to be completed later.
- **A fourth colour on a standard 300-pair model makes it 400 pairs** (4 × 100), not 4 × 75.
- **Lens colour minimums sit on top of the frame minimum.** Solid standard colours start at 100 pairs per colour, gradient at 150, mirror-coated at 300, and a custom colour outside the range at 300. A mirror-coated colourway therefore starts at 300 pairs even when the frame minimum would allow 100.
- **Existing tooling does not waive the minimum.** Reusing a catalogue construction saves tooling, not quantity.
- **Samples are for evaluation, not small-order supply.** They lead to a production order; they are not a way to buy 20 or 50 pairs.

One material detail changes the colour plan. Mazzucchelli acetate sheet carries a much higher raw-material minimum — enough material for roughly 1,000 pairs of one style in one colour — so a first order in Italian acetate is effectively one colour per style. With JIMEI or JINYU acetate, the three-colour split works from 100 pairs per colour.

## The sample fee is not the price of your order

This is the most common misreading we see. A custom sample is not a small production run. One frame carries the design work, tooling, material sourcing, custom components and manual processing that would otherwise be spread across hundreds of pairs, so the sample fee is much higher than a bulk unit price.

Eligible sample, development, tooling and 2D fees are credited against bulk payments once the same design reaches a cumulative 1,000 pairs within 12 months; freight is excluded. For TR-90 injection constructions, a separate and higher cumulative threshold applies and is stated in the quotation. When a first quotation looks expensive per pair, separate the one-time items from the recurring ones — that is the number that matters at volume.

## What you pay, and what comes back

Sample, development and tooling charges are paid in full before their stage begins; the factory does not carry development costs against a later order. Bulk production requires a 50% T/T deposit after sample approval, with the balance before shipment. Payments are in USD by bank transfer, unit prices are EXW by default, and shipping is by DHL or your nominated forwarder with freight, duties and taxes quoted separately.

Three terms reduce the risk of paying for development that goes nowhere:

- **Fee credit.** As above: eligible fees are credited once the same design reaches 1,000 pairs within 12 months, freight excluded. TR-90 follows its own threshold.
- **Stopping before tooling.** If you review the 2D drawings and stop before tooling begins, the 2D design work is retained and the balance is refunded to the original payment account within 10 working days, less a 0.5% transfer fee. The completed 2D drawings remain yours.
- **Tooling ownership.** Customer-paid molds and tooling belong to the customer. They normally stay at our factory for production, are never used for another customer, and can be released on request once all sums due are settled. Repeat production on the same design carries no new tooling charge while the tooling remains usable.

Once tooling begins, the mold is cut for your design and no refund applies.

## Ownership, exclusivity and confidentiality

Ownership follows payment, and it differs by route:

- **Drawings.** For a fully custom project developed from your design, the 2D drawings, renderings and production drawings belong to you and are not reused for another customer.
- **Tooling.** Fully paid tooling is yours, reserved for your confirmed design, stored at our factory for production unless you request its return.
- **Catalogue models and know-how.** These remain Kssmi's, which is why a catalogue-based private-label project cannot be exclusive.
- **NDA.** We can review and sign your NDA, or provide our mutual NDA on request, before confidential designs are shared.
- **Client confidentiality.** We do not confirm or discuss which brands we produce for.

If exclusivity matters, the route is a custom design with paid tooling: the mold is used only for your project. A catalogue shape with your logo on it cannot be made exclusive.

## FAQ

### 1. What is the difference between OEM, ODM and private label?

OEM means we manufacture to your confirmed design. ODM means we develop the design from your direction. Private label describes whose brand goes on the product and can apply to a catalogue-based or custom project.

### 2. Is OEM or ODM better for a new eyewear brand?

It depends on what you can send today. With a concept but no finished design, ODM is the shorter path because our team produces the drawing. With a confirmed specification, OEM avoids paying twice for design work. With neither, start from catalogue references and evaluate samples.

### 3. What is the minimum order?

Most custom acetate, metal and titanium models start at 300 pairs per model, normally split into up to three colourways of 100 pairs. Carbon fibre starts at 200 pairs per model. TR-90 requires 1,200 pairs per style, with at least 600 pairs per colour. The minimum is per model, not per order.

### 4. Can I split the minimum across several models or colours?

Across colours of the same model, yes: 300 pairs can be three colourways of 100. Across different models, no. Each model carries its own development and production setup, and a partial first batch is not produced to be completed later.

### 5. Is the sample fee the price of my order?

No. A custom sample concentrates one-off design, tooling and setup costs into a single frame, so it costs more than a bulk unit price. Eligible fees are credited against bulk payments once the same design reaches 1,000 pairs within 12 months, excluding freight; TR-90 follows a separate threshold.

### 6. Do I need CAD files or a designer to start?

No. A sketch, product photo, reference image or AI-generated concept is enough for engineering review. Our team produces the 2D technical drawing for your approval.

### 7. Who owns the mold and the drawings?

For a fully custom project based on your design, the drawings belong to you once development fees are paid, and fully paid tooling is yours, reserved for your project. Catalogue models and manufacturing know-how remain Kssmi's.

### 8. Can I get an exclusive design?

Yes, through a custom design with paid tooling: the mold is used only for your project. A catalogue construction used for private label remains available to other buyers and cannot be made exclusive.

### 9. Do you sell ready stock?

No. Catalogue models are references and evaluation samples; every production pair is made for a brand. Stock samples are for quality evaluation and lead to a production order.

### 10. Can you supply retail packaging or prescription lenses?

No. We do not manufacture or supply retail packaging, and we do not produce prescription lenses. Optical frames ship with demo lenses.
